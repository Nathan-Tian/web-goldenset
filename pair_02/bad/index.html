Overview はじめに
Kyoto is not a checklist city. It rewards the traveler who walks slowly, arrives early, and lets one place lead — by foot, by lantern light, by instinct — to the next.

Three days is enough to feel the city's pulse without pretending to know it. This guide strings together the eastern hills, the western valley of Arashiyama, and the northern path of philosophers into one unhurried loop — each day built around a single neighborhood, a single mood, and a handful of places you will remember longer than the photographs.

Every morning starts before the tour buses; every evening ends along the Kamo River or in a lantern-lit alley where the city exhales. Between dawn and dusk you will walk through ten thousand vermilion gates, sit with moss older than your country, and eat your way from market stall to kaiseki table.

“The best way to see Kyoto is to walk it — and to let the quiet parts speak first.”

01
Day one · Higashiyama 東山

Gates, gorges & the great wooden stage

Early start ~8 hrs 9.4 km Iconic firsts
08:00Fushimi Inari Taisha伏見稲荷大社1.5 hrs

Walk the senbon torii — ten thousand vermilion gates climbing Mount Inari. Few visitors reach the Yotsutsuji viewpoint; be one of them and watch the city wake below.

10:15Tōfuku-ji東福寺45 min

A Zen giant with a striped gravel garden and the Tsūtenkyō bridge over a valley of maple — extraordinary in November, serene any season.

11:30Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka二年坂・三年坂1 hr

Stone lanes of machiya townhouses tilted with age — pick up yatsuhashi and a hand-thrown cup. Go early to have the slope almost to yourself.

13:00Kiyomizu-dera清水寺2 hrs

The great stage, built without a single nail, juts over the maple valley. Drink from the Otowa waterfall — one sip for health, one for wisdom, one for love.

15:30Yasaka Shrine & Gion八坂神社・祇園2 hrs

Slip from the shrine's lantern courtyard into Hanamikoji. At dusk, maiko hurry between tea houses — the street glows exactly like the old woodblock prints.

18:30Kaiseki in Gion, then Pontochō先斗町evening

Dinner at a tucked-away counter, then drift into Pontochō — a lantern alley one person wide, with the Kamo River whispering at its end.

!Insider note: Fushimi Inari opens all night and is gloriously empty before 08:00. Reverse the tourist flow — gates first, temple second — and you will miss every queue.

02
Day two · Arashiyama 嵐山

Bamboo, a river & the moss that remembers

Moderate pace ~8 hrs 7.1 km The scenic west
08:00Arashiyama Bamboo Grove竹林の小径40 min

Before the crowds, the grove is cathedral-quiet. Wind moves through the stalks like a slow wooden instrument — stop, close your eyes, listen.

09:00Tenryū-ji & Ōkōchi Sansō天龍寺2 hrs

The Sōgenchi garden borrows whole mountains for its backdrop; the actor Ōkōchi's villa above it hides the valley's best view — and a free matcha with admission.

11:30Togetsukyō Bridge & Monkey Park渡月橋1.5 hrs

Cross the Moon-Crossing Bridge, then climb the short path to Iwatayama, where wild macaques groom each other above the whole river valley.

13:00Riverbank lunch湯豆腐1 hr

Yudofu — tofu simmered at the table in kelp broth — eaten while the river runs past. Kyoto's most meditative meal.

14:30Giō-ji & Saga-Toriimoto祇王寺2 hrs

A thatched hermitage drowning in carpet moss, then a preserved Meiji street of tea houses — stop for sencha and a Kyoto sweet before the ride back.

16:45Randen tram home嵐電30 min

The little purple Randen rattles through neighborhoods and under persimmon trees — golden hour from a tram window is the day's final scene.

!Insider note: Optional detour — if legs allow, ride one stop past the tram's end and bus to Kinkaku-ji before its 17:00 close. The gold pavilion at dusk is worth the scramble.

Day two terrainSoft gravel paths · a short mountain climb · tram tracks — the west is gentler underfoot.
03
Day three · North to downtown 哲学の道

Philosophers, silver sand & the kitchen of Kyoto

Slow & contemplative ~9 hrs 10.5 km Taste the city
08:30Ginkaku-ji銀閣寺1.5 hrs

The Silver Pavilion that was never silvered — raked sand waves, a cone of moonlight, and a mirror pond holding it all. Quietest of the great temples.

10:00Philosopher's Path哲学の道1 hr

A two-kilometer canal walk lined with cherry, moss and stone Buddhas. In autumn the maples lean over the water; in any season it thinks for you.

11:00Hōnen-in & Nanzen-ji法然院・南禅寺1.5 hrs

Hōnen-in's moss gate is the city's best-kept secret. Nanzen-ji hides a Meiji aqueduct striding over the temple like a Roman bridge — and superb coffee in a former bathhouse.

13:00Nishiki Market crawl錦市場2 hrs

“Kyoto's kitchen” — five blocks of pickles, dashi, tamago, wagashi and knives. Eat as you walk: grilled mochi, yuba, a tiny cup of sake at a counter no wider than a door.

15:00Machiya tea ceremony茶の湯1 hr

Your last Kyoto afternoon, spent kneeling at a lacquer table while a tea master whisks koicha in a 300-year-old townhouse. Booked in advance; unforgettable always.

18:00Farewell along the Kamo鴨川evening

Dinner on a kawadoko deck above the river — or a last lantern-lit wander through Gion. Say goodbye the way Kyoto prefers: slowly, over one more cup.

!Insider note: Swap day three for Uji if tea is your religion — the matcha town and Byōdō-in's phoenix hall are a 30-minute train ride, and the whole day unfolds beside the river.
Silver Pavilion · Ginkaku-ji
Day three terrainCanal paths · market tile · tatami floors — the easiest walking of the trip, the fullest day of flavor.
Field notes 名所

Worth the detour

Beyond the three-day spine, these places earn their own chapter. Each is an easy half-day from downtown — choose the ones that call to you.

Kinkaku-ji金閣寺

North-west · 40 min bus

The Golden Pavilion, wrapped in real gold leaf, floats above its mirror pond. Visit late afternoon when the west light sets the roof on fire.

The most photographed building in Kyoto — and it earns it

Nijō-jo二条城

Central · 25 min walk from downtown

The shogun's castle of whispering floors — nightingale boards that sing at every step, walls painted in gold leaf, gardens designed to intimidate.

History you can hear under your feet

Byōdō-in, Uji平等院

Uji · 30 min train

The Phoenix Hall glides on its pond like a ship from a sutra — the image on the ¥10 coin. Pair it with matcha in the town that invented it.

Heian Japan's surviving masterpiece — and the best tea in the country

Kennin-ji建仁寺

Gion · 10 min walk

Kyoto's oldest Zen temple hides the twin dragons painted across its ceiling and a calm rock garden steps from Gion's bustle.

Zen silence two minutes from the crowds

Heian Shrine garden平安神宮

East · 20 min walk

A giant vermilion torii opens onto a Meiji-era garden of weeping cherries and irises — the shrine's lake stroll is spectacular in bloom.

The torii is the city's tallest; the garden is its most romantic

Kibune & Kurama貴船・鞍馬

North · 60 min train

Two mountain villages joined by a forest trail: riverside dining on stilts in Kibune, and Kurama's mountain temple reached through giant cedar.

Kyoto's coolest escape — literally, by several degrees

Kyoto's highest art: twelve courses of season and silence — lacquer, ceramic, a single maple leaf on the sashimi. Book a counter seat; watch the chef compose.

May–September only — Kyoto's warm-season ritual
Step inside 文化

Not just to see — to do

Kyoto reveals itself through its rituals. These four experiences turn watching into participation — each one bookable, each one worth the early planning.

No. 01

Tea ceremony

Chanoyu · 60 min · from ¥4,500

Kneel at a lacquer table in a 300-year-old machiya while a tea master moves through a ritual perfected over four centuries. You'll whisk your own bowl — and taste the difference.

Book 3–5 days ahead
No. 02

Morning zazen

Sitting meditation · 90 min · donation ¥2,000+

Face a wall in a 800-year-old Zen hall and sit with the monks. No experience needed — only ten minutes of your ego. The tea afterward tastes like it was invented for you.

Arrive 30 min early
No. 03

Kimono & Gion stroll

Dress & walk · 3 hrs · from ¥6,000

Spend an afternoon in silk: a kimono stylist dresses you layer by layer, then you walk Hanamikoji at maiko pace — small steps, straight back, suddenly part of the scenery.

Book 1 week ahead
No. 04

Ryokan evening

Yukata, bath & feast · from ¥25,000 pp

Trade the hotel for a wooden inn: a deep cypress bath, dinner served course by course in your room, futons laid while you're away. Kyoto's most complete ritual of rest.

Book 1–2 months ahead
Practical 心得

The know-before-you-go file

Six questions every first-time visitor asks. Open each when you need it.

Getting around 移動 ＋
  • IC card first. ICOCA / Suica / PASMO all work on Kyoto buses, subways and trains — tap in, tap out, reload at any station.
  • Bus over subway for tourists. The city bus reaches every temple; one-day pass ¥700 (or ¥1,100 for the subway+bus combo) pays for itself after three rides.
  • The loop route 100/101/102 circles the main sights. Buses board from the rear in Kyoto and exit at the front — pay as you leave.
  • Taxis are clean, metered and abundant after 21:00 when buses thin — short hops ¥900–1,500.
Money & cards お金 ＋
  • Cash is still kind. Temple gates, market stalls and tiny noodle counters often take cash only — carry ¥10,000–20,000 per day.
  • Cards work almost everywhere else — including most buses and all major restaurants, and contactless is standard.
  • ATMs: Seven Bank (7-Eleven), Japan Post and Lawson accept international cards around the clock.
  • Tipping is not a thing — service is included and excellent. Leave nothing; say a warm ありがとう instead.
Temple etiquette マナー ＋
  • Photography rules vary. Most gardens welcome it; many interiors forbid it. If you see a crossed-out camera, put yours away completely.
  • Remove shoes where asked — step in sock feet, place shoes neatly, and never point the soles at the altar.
  • At shrines: bow twice, clap twice, bow once. At the water pavilion, rinse left hand, right hand, then mouth — never drink from the ladle directly.
  • Quiet is reverence. Temples are living places of worship, not museums. Voices low, phones silent, incense respected.
When & weather 季節 ＋
  • Mid-November to early December is the maple climax — this guide assumes it — expect crowds by 10:00 and book dinner ahead.
  • March–April trades maples for cherry blossom and the same crowds; July–August is hot, humid and gloriously quiet.
  • Rain is a gift here. Wet moss glows, empty temples feel sacred, and a folding umbrella + a machiya tea house turn bad weather into the best hour of the trip.
  • Temples open 08:00–17:00 on average and often stop entry 30 min early — always confirm the day before at your hotel desk.
Staying connected 通信 ＋
  • eSIM first. Install a Japan eSIM before you fly (Airalo, Ubigi) — 5–10 GB covers maps, translation and social for a week.
  • Free Wi-Fi is available at most convenience stores, department stores and major stations, plus Kyoto City Wi-Fi around key sights.
  • Download offline maps of Higashiyama and Arashiyama — the lanes are easy to wander and easy to misplace yourself in, beautifully.
  • Navigation: Google Maps works reliably for buses and trains; the Kyoto Bus Navi app gives live bus positions.
A few phrases 言葉 ＋
  • ありがとうございます — arigatō gozaimasu — thank you (use it constantly, with a small bow)
  • すみません — sumimasen — excuse me / sorry / the universal opener
  • おいしい — oishii — delicious (say it after every meal; cooks beam)
  • いくらですか — ikura desu ka — how much?
  • 英語が話せますか — eigo ga hanasemasu ka — do you speak English?
